Angular Statistics Meets New Galaxy Surveys

IFPU Focused Week

12–16 October 2026, Trieste, Italy

Scientific Rationale

Ongoing and upcoming galaxy surveys, including DESI, Euclid, SPHEREx, Roman, and Rubin/LSST, are opening a new regime for studies of large-scale structure. By mapping ever larger cosmic volumes across broad redshift ranges, these experiments will deliver measurements of galaxy clustering and gravitational lensing with unprecedented precision. Fully realising their scientific potential will require theoretical predictions and analysis methods that match both the accuracy and complexity of the data. It will also require combining spectroscopic and photometric galaxy samples with complementary CMB observations, improving control of systematics, breaking parameter degeneracies, and testing the consistency of the cosmological model. Angular observables, particularly the angular power spectrum and bispectrum, offer a natural framework for such joint analyses. They can incorporate survey geometry, projection effects, radial evolution, and relativistic contributions, and connect galaxy clustering and lensing with CMB lensing and other projected probes. Yet the relevant theory, estimators, and numerical pipelines remain fragmented, especially for spectroscopic surveys, higher-order statistics, and very large scales. Addressing these challenges is essential for extracting information about primordial non-Gaussianity, the growth of structure, relativistic effects, and possible departures from the standard cosmological model. This Focus Week will bring together experts in theory, phenomenology, and data analysis to assess current methods, compare full- and flat-sky approaches, identify conceptual and computational bottlenecks, and develop a roadmap towards robust, survey-ready angular analyses. The programme will emphasise discussion, benchmark problems, and collaborative work across the large-scale-structure and CMB communities.
